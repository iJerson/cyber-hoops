# Dribble Biomechanics Reference

Purpose: ground-truth reference for procedural dribble animation. Focus is natural, functional dribbling — not flashy moves. Each section: what the body does, and why it reads as natural.

## Core principles (apply everywhere)

- **Posture:** athletic stance. Knees bent 20–40°, hips hinged back, chest over toes, spine neutral — never upright, never hunched. The lower the pressure, the lower the stance.
- **Center of gravity (COG):** always between the feet, slightly toward the balls of the feet. The ball is dribbled *around* a stable COG; the COG never chases the ball.
- **Ball height:** waist or below in traffic; up to hip when open. Rule: higher dribble = faster movement but more exposed. Idle protected dribble ≈ knee height; open floor speed dribble ≈ waist/hip.
- **Hand:** on *top* of the ball at contact (palm down, fingers spread). Palm never slaps — contact is finger pads and pad of palm. The hand *rides* the ball up ~1/3 of its rise, then pushes it down. Between contacts the hand hovers, wrist cocked.
- **Wrist:** does the pumping. Elbow moves a little, shoulder barely. Snap down on the push, relax on the ride-up.
- **Fingers:** last touch and first touch. They aim the ball; direction changes come from fingertips rolling to the side of the ball, not from the arm.
- **Rhythm:** dribble tempo locks to gait — usually one bounce per step or per two steps. Desync between feet and bounce is what makes bad animation look wrong.
- **Eyes:** up. Head level, scanning. Never watching the ball — peripheral vision tracks it. Head down = beginner.
- **Free arm:** live, not hanging — half-raised bar between ball and defender, or counter-swinging in the run.

## Walking dribble

- Torso lean ~5–10° forward, easy knee bend.
- Ball bounces beside the front of the lead foot, slightly outside the hip line — not directly in front (kicking risk), not behind.
- One bounce per step or per two steps; contact happens as the same-side foot plants — the body's downward pulse and the push-down share timing.
- Shoulders level, small natural counter-rotation with the stride.
- Wrist does nearly all the work; upper arm swings maybe 15–20°.
- Looks natural because: bounce rhythm and stride rhythm are phase-locked; the ball never crosses the body's midline.

## Running dribble (speed dribble)

- Torso lean increases with speed (10–20°). COG drops slightly.
- Ball pushed *forward*, not down — out ahead of the body at waist-to-chest bounce height so the player runs onto it. One bounce per 2–3 strides.
- The arm extends more; the push is forward-and-down at ~45°.
- Free arm pumps in normal running counter-swing.
- Hips and shoulders face the direction of travel; almost no rotation.
- Looks natural because: the ball leads the runner like a passed baton — body posture is a sprinter's, with one arm interrupted per bounce.

## Protecting the ball (shield stance)

- Body turns *sideways* to the defender — the dribbling side goes far, the free shoulder and forearm form a wall.
- Stance widens; knees bend deeper; COG drops noticeably; ball height drops to knee.
- Ball dribbled on the far hip, slightly behind the body plane; head over the front shoulder, eyes on defender.
- Dribble tempo quickens (short low bounces = less steal window).
- Looks natural because: mass between ball and threat, low fast bounce, visible tension in wide stance.

## Changing direction

- Deceleration step first: the outside foot plants wide, knee bends, COG shifts over the plant leg.
- Ball is pulled with fingertips to the other side *during* the plant — hand rolls from top of ball to its outer side and pushes across.
- Hips rotate first, shoulders follow, then the ball is already moving to the new line.
- Looks natural because: sequencing is plant → hip turn → ball swap → push-off. If the ball changes sides before the plant, it reads fake.

## Explosive acceleration (first step)

- Preceded by a lower coil: extra knee bend, torso drops.
- First dribble of the burst is long and hard, thrown out ahead; body chases it.
- Back leg drives, torso lean jumps to 20°+, free arm rips through like a sprinter start.
- Ball height on that first push is high (hip/waist) because it must travel far.
- Looks natural because: ball leaves *before* the body — you throw the ball into space, then run through it.

## Stopping

- Two-step brake: first foot lands ahead of COG (heel-ish), second closes; knees soak the momentum, torso stays over feet (never rocking back).
- Simultaneously, dribble either dies to a low rapid protect bounce or is picked up.
- Shoulders raise slightly out of the run lean back to neutral.
- Looks natural because: the ball's bounce height decays with the body's deceleration — both "lose energy" together.

## Crossover

- Setup bounce slightly higher/wider to sell direction.
- Hand rolls over the top-outside of the ball; push is diagonal — across the body and *back-to-front* low (below knee for a tight cross).
- Simultaneous: wide plant of the outside foot, hard hip snap to the new side, shoulders dip toward the fake side then whip to the real side.
- Receiving hand takes the ball out front on the other side, in step with the opposite foot.
- Looks natural because: ball, hips, shoulders arrive on the new side in that order within ~one bounce; the low bounce clears the defender's reach.

## Behind-the-back

- Used mid-stride when a defender takes the front lane.
- Ball is wrapped around the back with the wrist and fingers — the arm sweeps behind the hip, releasing so the bounce lands beside the *opposite* foot as it strides.
- Torso stays forward-facing and upright; the move hides in the gait — no shoulder telegraph.
- Slight hip drop during the wrap to shorten the path.
- Looks natural because: gait doesn't break; only the arm path changes; the ball reappears synced to the next footfall.

## Spin move

- Plant on the inside foot, reverse pivot 180–270°; ball is *pulled* on the hip through the spin, glued to the hand by centripetal wrap (no push — a drag).
- Head whips around early to re-acquire vision before the body finishes.
- COG stays centered over the pivot foot; exit dribble pushed into the new lane on the first step out.
- Looks natural because: pivot foot never slides, head leads the rotation, ball hugs the body's radius the whole way round.

## Animation checklist for Cyber Hoops

1. Bounce phase must lock to gait phase (one bounce per step idle/walk; per 2–3 strides at sprint).
2. Hand meets ball at top of its rise, rides ~1/3, pushes; wrist-led, not shoulder-led.
3. Ball placement: front-outside of the dribbling-side foot (walk), thrown ahead (run), far hip + low (protected).
4. Torso lean scales with speed; crouch scales with pressure.
5. Free arm never idle: counter-swing (run) or shield bar (protect).
6. Head up always.
7. Direction change order: foot plant → hips → shoulders → ball → push-off.
8. Bounce height decays with deceleration, grows with the first step of acceleration.
