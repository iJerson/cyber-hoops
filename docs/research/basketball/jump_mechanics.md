# Jump Mechanics

## Countermovement jump (the standard)

Sequence: dip (hips/knees flex, arms swing back, ~150–250ms) → triple extension
(ankle+knee+hip fire together) → arm swing up → takeoff. The dip's
stretch-shortening cycle adds ~10–20% height versus a squat jump; the arm swing
adds another ~10%. Nobody skips either.

## One-foot vs two-foot takeoff

- **One-foot (off the run):** converts horizontal speed to height; longer, flatter
  jump arc; used for layups and running dunks. Last two steps: long penultimate
  (drops the hips), short final plant.
- **Two-foot (power jump):** slower to load, more controlled, stronger in traffic;
  jump-stop then explode; used for power dunks, blocks, rebounds.

## Flight

- Pure ballistics: COG follows a parabola no mid-air effort can change. Apex hang:
  ~71% of flight time is spent in the top half of the arc — the real "hang time"
  illusion. Elite verticals 0.7–1.1m → total flight 0.75–0.95s.
- Mid-air posture changes (tucking legs, arching) move limbs *around* the COG —
  the head can stay level at apex while legs tuck, selling extra hang.

## Landing

- Absorb through toes → ball of foot → heel, knees flexing 30–60°; landing force
  2–4× bodyweight. Stiff-leg landings look wrong instantly.
- Two-foot landings after airborne actions; single-foot landing reads off-balance
  (usable as a "wild finish" tell).

## Game application (Cyber Hoops)

- Pre-load dip already specced (arcade-compressed 50–70ms).
- Dunk hang-time exaggeration = stretch the top half of the parabola (where real
  hang already lives) — physically-flavored cheat, reads natural.
- Layup should take off one-foot from the gather's second step; dunk in traffic
  two-foot. Currently both are scripted lunges — takeoff style is the visible
  difference.
- Landing knee absorption + squash already specced; add the toe-first foot pitch
  when Foot IK lands.
