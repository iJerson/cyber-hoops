# Layups

## The gather (universal)

Two-step rhythm off the dribble: ball secured during step 1 (outside foot),
step 2 (inside foot) is the launch. Right-hand layup: gather on right foot,
jump off *left* foot, right knee drives up — knee drive and shooting arm rise
together (same-side coordination, opposite leg takeoff).

## Finishing mechanics

- **Overhand (push) finish:** palm behind the ball, extended high; stronger
  through contact; standard at speed.
- **Underhand (finger roll):** palm up, ball rolled off fingertips; softest
  touch, kills speed's effect on the ball.
- Ball carried up the body midline → extended at full reach; release at the top
  of reach, ~3–3.3m for pros — above rim-level interference.

## Backboard use

- From either side at an angle: aim the upper corner of the painter's square,
  ball banks and drops. The 45° window is the highest-percentage finish in
  basketball.
- Straight-on drives: rim finish or high-off-glass; board angle too shallow.
- Spin: layup released with the board hit inherits soft backspin from the roll
  off the fingers — deadens the bank.

## Speed and body control

- Elite finishers *decelerate into control* on the last two steps — the gather
  eats speed so the release is soft even off a sprint.
- Body shields the ball: layup arm is the far arm from the defender, body
  between (same principle as the protect dribble).

## Game application (Cyber Hoops)

- Current layup = low-arc solved shot. Upgrade path: gather 1-2 step → one-foot
  takeoff → high release point (raise release height to ~2.6m during layup) →
  bank targeting when approach angle is 30–60° off the board.
- Bank shot solver: aim point on the board (upper square corner) instead of rim
  center; ballistic solve identical, target swapped + reflection.
- Finish arm should be chosen by defender side (shield rule) once contested
  finishes matter.
